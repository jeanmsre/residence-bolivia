Your residency

The right residency path for your situation

Bolivian law requires you to show a specific purpose for being in the country (an "objeto determinado"): a pension, a business activity, a contract. Depending on your profile, two main paths are open to you. We confirm the right one during the call.

Temporary residency · 1 year

The simple way to start

Who it's for: you don't yet have income or an activity to present in Bolivia, or you want a legal status to buy and manage your affairs locally.
  • Requested with a sworn statement
  • Bolivian foreign resident ID card
  • Renewable, with a possible switch to the 3-year path
Ideal for retirees
Temporary residency · 3 years

The most direct path

Who it's for: you already have a qualifying purpose, such as a retirement pension, a business activity or an employment contract.
  • Three worry-free years, with no yearly renewal
  • Bolivian foreign resident ID card
  • Opens the way to permanent residency
Temporary residency · 3 years

Through an introduction

Who it's for: you don't have a qualifying purpose yet and would like us to look within our network for a company or person who needs your skills.
  • Introductions to trusted local contacts
  • Any agreement is made directly between you and them
  • Same benefits as the 3-year path

Where to live in Bolivia

Santa Cruz, the city where life happens outdoors all year round

Bolivia's economic capital, Santa Cruz de la Sierra brings together what many people look for in the next chapter of their lives: warmth, modern and green neighborhoods, excellent restaurants, buildings with pools and a cost of living that finally gives you room to breathe.

With a much lower cost of living (see the comparison), your pension or savings go further. And at about 400 meters (1,300 feet) above sea level, far from the heights of La Paz, life moves at a gentle pace in a green, spread-out city.

Who can get residency in Bolivia?

Bolivian law requires you to show a specific purpose for being in the country (an "objeto determinado"): a pension, a job, a business activity or studies. If you have one, the 3-year residency is open to you. If not, the 1-year residency is requested with a sworn statement. We confirm the right path for you during the free call.

Is a retirement pension enough?

Yes, a retirement pension can support the 3-year residency. You need to provide official proof of it: we tell you exactly which document, based on your country and your pension provider.

Do I have to live in Santa Cruz?

No. Your paperwork is done with us in Santa Cruz, but your Bolivian residency is valid throughout the country. Cochabamba, Sucre, Tarija or Samaipata: we help you compare cities by budget, climate and altitude.

How long do I need to stay in Bolivia for the process?

Plan on about 15 to 21 days in Santa Cruz: medical exam, Interpol certificate, immigration filing, then your foreign resident ID card. This estimate is based on our experience with complete documents; the authorities set the pace.

Which documents do I need to prepare?

A passport valid for at least 6 months, then documents that depend on your profile (for example, proof of pension or income). Foreign documents must be apostilled, then officially translated into Spanish in Bolivia: translations are included. After the call, you receive a checklist tailored to your country.

I don't speak Spanish. Is that a problem?

Not at all. We work with you in English, and someone who speaks English and Spanish is by your side at every official appointment.

How much does your service cost?

Our fee depends on your profile and your residency path. We share it during the free call, and it is then written into your service agreement. The package includes government fees and translations, all the way to your Bolivian ID card. Settling-in services (airport pickup, housing, bank account, NIT tax number, driver's license) are optional, each with a price shared in advance.

How does payment work?

In two installments: 50% when you sign your service agreement, 50% when you arrive in Santa Cruz. Accepted payment methods (including international bank transfer) are listed in your agreement.

Can my family come with me?

Yes. Each family member has their own file; we organize the process so you complete it together, during the same stay.

Can I travel outside Bolivia during my residency?

Yes. Presence rules apply depending on your type of residency. We explain them clearly before each trip and take care of any required requests.

Can I buy an apartment without being a resident?

Yes, a foreigner can buy with a passport (except within 50 km (31 mi) of the borders). Residency is still useful day to day: bank account, tax number, renting out your property. See our real estate page.

What happens after temporary residency?

You keep lifetime access to our private WhatsApp group, for clients only, for your everyday questions. After 3 years, you can apply for permanent residency, then, under the conditions set by law, Bolivian citizenship. We support you at every step, as well as with your driver's license and Ciudadanía Digital (the digital ID for online government services).

Who handles the legal work?

Legal and notarial acts are carried out by lawyers and notaries registered in Bolivia, whom we work with. We coordinate everything and remain your single point of contact, from start to finish.

Can you advise me on taxes?

We do not give tax advice. For these questions, we connect you with an independent accountant in Bolivia, and we recommend you also consult a specialist in your home country.
